During outbreaks, clinicians often have to decide at the bedside whether a patient likely has mpox, before laboratory confirmation is available. This project asks what machine learning can and cannot add to that decision.

I am leading a prespecified evaluation, reported following TRIPOD+AI, of six types of machine learning models for bedside diagnosis of clade Ib mpox in 455 patients from eastern DR Congo, using nested cross-validation and temporal validation. The models reached an AUROC of 0.70–0.71, compared with 0.51 for the national case definition. However, no model met the criteria for clinical use, and model calibration did not transfer. I am now extending the work to lesion-image and multimodal models.

The manuscript is in preparation.
